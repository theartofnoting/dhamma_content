1. Stopping the Stream of Proliferation
---------------------------------------

When the eye sees a visible form or the ear hears a sound, the untrained mind immediately runs outward in pursuit of stories, judgments, and desires. Mogok Sayadaw compares this habit to a traveler running blindly down a slope without ever pausing to inspect where each step lands.

"Stopping" (samatha in its practical sense) begins the moment you refuse to follow the story. Instead of adding a second thought of liking or disliking onto the first contact, you steady the mind right at the point of awareness. In that pause, restlessness settles and clarity becomes possible.

2. Contemplating Arising and Passing Away
-----------------------------------------

Stopping alone is not yet the end of the path; it prepares the clear mirror for contemplation (vipassanā). Once the mind stops chasing external concepts, turn attention directly to the consciousness or sensation currently present—hearing, thinking, warmth, tightness, or pleasant feeling.

Watch carefully: did that state stay permanent even for two heartbeats? No—it arose from conditions and vanished right where it stood. When you directly discern impermanence (anicca) in the present mind-and-matter process, ignorance loses its footing and craving cannot bind the heart.
